steps:
  - title: The rhubarb
    body: |-
      Peel the two rhubarb stalks. Cut one stalk into four 10 cm pieces.
      Poach the four pieces in a mixture of grenadine syrup and water (50 g grenadine to 100 g water). After 5 minutes, remove two of the pieces. Continue cooking the remaining two until they break down into a purée. Reserve the remaining poaching liquid.
  - title: The rhubarb
    body: Wrap the two poached rhubarb stalks tightly in cling film to form an even cylinder. Chill in the refrigerator, ideally overnight, or at least until completely cold. Remove the cling film and cut into diagonal pieces. Just before serving, reheat the grenadine caramel with a little water and spoon it over the rhubarb.
  - title: The rhubarb
    body: 'Divide the second raw rhubarb stalk into two preparations: cut one part into a 2 × 10 mm brunoise to mix with the peas, and shave the other into thin ribbons. Reserve the ribbons for plating and lightly torch them just before serving.'
  - title: The peas
    body: Blanch the peas for just 30 seconds, then immediately transfer them to iced water to stop the cooking.
  - title: The peas
    body: Mix equal parts rhubarb brunoise and peas with a little pea pod vinaigrette. Set aside.
  - title: Pea pod vinaigrette
    body: Blend 300 g of pea pods with 150 ml of water. Add 60 g of neutral oil, the fresh lemon verbena (reserve the small leaves for garnish), lemon juice, salt and pepper, then blend until smooth. Strain through a fine sieve.
  - title: Haddock
    body: Trim the haddock fillet, reserving the best portion for serving and the rest for the mousse. Slice the serving portion on the bias, then lightly torch it to enhance its smoky flavour.
  - title: Haddock cream
    body: Cover the haddock trimmings (80 g) with the cream (150 g) and gently cook until just done. Blend until smooth, then pass through a fine sieve.
  - title: Finishing touches
    body: Remove the rhubarb stalk from the refrigerator, unwrap it, and cut it into diagonal pieces.
  - title: Finishing touches
    body: Reheat the grenadine caramel with a little water, then glaze the poached rhubarb pieces.
  - title: Haddock mousse
    body: Just before serving, bring 50 g of milk and 50 g of butter to a gentle boil, keeping a close eye on it. Add to the haddock cream, then blend with an immersion blender, tilting the saucepan slightly to create a light, airy foam.
  - title: Finishing touches
    body: Plate the pea and rhubarb brunoise, the glazed rhubarb pieces and the haddock. Add the haddock foam at the last moment, then finish with the pea pod vinaigrette and fresh lemon verbena leaves.

tips:
  - title: Fresh peas are essential
    body: Frozen peas are not suitable, as the freezing process alters their texture and they turn brown and soft. Fresh peas need just 30 seconds of cooking.
  - title: The foam technique
    body: The milk creates the foam, while the butter gives it stability. Use an immersion blender, tilting the saucepan slightly and blending near the surface without touching the bottom. Work quickly between making the foam and plating the dish.
  - title: Wrapping the rhubarb cylinder in cling film
    body: The cling film helps the rhubarb keep its shape, making it easier to cut clean, even pieces. If it is not fully chilled, leave the cling film on until just before slicing. Ideally, chill it overnight in the refrigerator.
